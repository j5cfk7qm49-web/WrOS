# Getting started

WrOS is a writing desk for one book at a time. The book is a folder of plain Markdown files, so everything you write can be read, copied and backed up without WrOS. You can have as many books as you like: *File › Start a new book…* starts another, and *File › Open another book…* switches (see *Your books*).

## The desk

- **The outline**, on the left, headed by the book's title: parts, chapters and scenes, then the notebook (Characters, Places, Notes, Research) and the rest of the book's sections.
- **The page**, on the right: the scene you're writing.
- **The Pomodoro**, under the outline: a timer drawn as a little world. `←` `→` turns it into the Visualizer.
- **Music**, below that, when you've turned it on.
- **The menu bar**, along the top, with whether your words are saved and when the book was last backed up at its right.
- **The status bar**, along the bottom: the book's words against its target, what you've written today and messages, then Wi-Fi, volume, battery and the time.

## Getting around

- `Tab` and `Shift-Tab` move between the panes.
- `Esc` lights **the menu bar** along the top, from anywhere; a menu's underlined letter (or `↓`) opens it, and everything WrOS can do is a step or two away. The highlighted row says what it does on the status line, and `?` opens the help on it (see *The menu bar, menu by menu*). `Esc` again closes it and leaves you where you were. `F10` opens the bar too, and so does a click on a menu's name.
- `Ctrl-K`, or a tap of `Super`, **finds anything**: every action, scene, note and theme by a few letters of its name, with the key that does the same thing and the menu it lives in.
- `?` or `F1` opens this help, on the topic for wherever you are. On the page, and in any box you type into, `?` is just a question mark, so use `F1` there.

On a Mac, where the terminal can send it, `Cmd` works everywhere `Ctrl` does.

## Saving and quitting

You never have to save. A scene is written two seconds after you stop typing, and never waits longer than twenty seconds. `Ctrl-S` saves at once if you like.

To quit: `Ctrl-Q`, `q` in the outline, or *WrOS › Quit*. Everything is saved first. If a save can't happen (a full disk, say), WrOS stays open and says so; `Ctrl-Q` again leaves anyway, and the words wait in recovery for next time.

## The mouse

Click a pane to move to it, a chapter to fold it, a scene to open it, and the prose to put the cursor there. Drag across the prose to select. The wheel scrolls whatever is under the pointer.

## About WrOS

*WrOS › About WrOS* (or `Ctrl-K` › *About WrOS*) shows what WrOS is built on and who made it: WrOS is built on GrimoireTUI, by Josh King and Catfinity Studios. `Enter`, or a click on the link, opens catfinity.com in your browser, and `l` opens the License topic. Catfinity Studios also makes **Catfinity**, an idle cat RPG for iPhone and Android: `c` in *About WrOS…* (or a click on its line) opens its page. From a terminal, `grimoire --version` says the same.

## Donate

GrimoireTUI, which WrOS is built on, is free, and a donation is never required. It's always appreciated, though, and it all goes toward GrimoireTUI's development. *Help › Donate…* (or `Ctrl-K` › *Donate (Ko-fi)*, or `d` in *About WrOS…*) opens the Ko-fi page in your browser.
