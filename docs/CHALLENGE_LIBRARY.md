# Offline challenge library: source research and acceptance design

**Current: researched/design stage. There is no bundled 1,000-plot Story Spark corpus yet.** The original tutorial instructions and two demo scenes are not counted as such a corpus.

## Rights-first acquisition

Candidate decisions are recorded in [CONTENT_LICENSES](CONTENT_LICENSES.md) and machine-readable `content-licenses.json`. Public-domain status is territorial and edition-specific. Modern introductions, illustrations, exercises credited to another author and publisher additions are not automatically covered by a book's general license. Sources not ingested are explicitly `bundled: false`.

Preferred paths:

- Precisely identified US-public-domain Cook/Polti/historical text excerpts with original-source identifiers and required provenance.
- Independently authored prompts based on general uncopyrightable methods, not copied modern lists.
- Properly attributed CC BY adaptations after chapter-level exceptions are reviewed.
- CC BY-SA material only in a clearly separated, appropriately ShareAlike-licensed adapted-content collection; MIT application code does not relicense the content.

## Record format (planned)

Each plot/exercise needs stable ID, record kind, original wording, source/edition/section identifier, adaptation status, license expression, attribution/notice, difficulty/duration/genre/category tags, narrative conditions and compatibility exclusions. A content manifest maps bundled file hashes to exact upstream sources. A compositional result records its component IDs and corpus version for history/replay.

## Count and quality gate

At least 1,000 substantively distinct curated plot situations or plot-generating options; independently useful creative exercises across dialogue, description, viewpoint, voice, structure, motivation, senses, pacing, setting, conflict, subtext, revision and formal experimentation. Distinct causal dilemmas/obstacles/stakes count; adjective/name swaps and a Cartesian product of near-identical templates do not.

Validate stable unique IDs, duplicate and near-duplicate wording, source coverage and license presence. Conduct an editorial sample review and compatibility review. Do not make a numeric completeness claim based only on generated JSON row counts.

## Constraint traditions

Lipograms, N+7, snowball sentences, restricted vocabulary, viewpoint restrictions, narrative reversal, formal dialogue, sensory limitation and temporal restructuring can be independently implemented with original explanations. A general method is different from copying a protected exercise description. An N+7 implementation also needs a clearly defined, licensed lexical ordering and a reversible experimental copy, not a silent rewrite of the current scene.

## Integration and tutorial

Challenge records support manual qualitative completion and measurable objectives separately. Add a result to an existing pin/scratchpad/task/scene or start a sprint only when those subsystems exist. Tutorial lessons 21–24 remain explicitly planned until actual corpus/generator/actions are implemented and tested. They cannot currently be marked complete.

## M3b does not change corpus completeness

The independent editor and safe-demo lesson 13 are now implemented/tested. They add no third-party exercise or plot corpus. Corpus size, edition clearance, compatibility and editorial quality gates above remain open; no test count or tutorial paragraph is counted as a curated Spark option.
